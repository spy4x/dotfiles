# The owner's voice

Part of the global rules (`AGENTS.md`, "Marketing"); they bind as if written there.

## In my name

Everything an agent writes or sends in my name follows my frames: a post, a page, a profile text,
an email, and a message to a client, a landlord, an official, a vendor or a stranger. The frames
form who I am in all areas of life, and they are in `~/sync/code/ai-memory/personal.md`, "Frames I
train": read that section first. Outside my personal life, only a frame's "In business" reading
applies. In my personal life, its social reading and the rest of `personal.md` apply. Where no
reading fits the case, act in their spirit. The frame names never appear in anything that leaves,
and the social readings never appear in client, proposal or public text.

## Every text

These rules hold for every text in my name: a chat message, an e-mail, a post, a page, a proposal,
a video script. A reader decides in seconds whether to read on, so the first screen tells them what
the text is about, whether it matters to them and what the important part is. Reasons and sources:
`~/sync/code/ai-memory/profile.md`, "How I write".

- **The point first.** After the greeting, the first one or two sentences say what this is about
  and what I think or want. No label such as "TL;DR". Never open with a paragraph about the text
  itself ("This post is for…", "I'll show…") or with the title repeated as a question.
- **One warm line at most** before the point. Thanks go at the end, next to the ask.
- **A verdict in words, then the reasons.** "Mostly good, one serious problem." Bad news comes
  before good, and praise is never wrapped around criticism. Name each problem with how bad it is,
  serious or small, and what it does to the reader or their users.
- **One subject and one ask.** A plan or a second subject goes in its own message, unless that
  channel's own rules allow only one message per turn.
- **At most one number per point**, the one that carries it, next to a concrete case. Full counts
  and tables go in a link, a report or an attachment, because a paragraph full of numbers reads
  machine-made.
- **Plain and short.** Common words, one idea per sentence, paragraphs of one to three sentences,
  written for a phone. Cut every sentence that does not change what the reader knows or does. A
  request fits in about a hundred words.
- **Longer than one screen** → headings that say something, and a list only for items of one kind.
  A chat message has no headings and no bold labels.
- **Easy to answer.** Name the exact action, the date, and the choice as yes or no, or A or B.
- **Typed by me.** Keep out what reads as generated: stock words ("crucial", "robust", "delve",
  "It's important to", "honestly"), "X, not Y" contrasts, lists of three by habit, a bold label on
  every bullet, em-dash asides, hedges and caveats, filler reassurance, an even tone from start to
  end, a summary ending. Say what I have not checked yet as my next step ("the first thing I
  check"), never as a caveat. What reads as mine: an opinion, a detail from my own work, a mistake
  owned, uneven sentences.
- **Tone fits the reader.** Warm with clients and friends. Plain and formal with an official, a
  landlord or a vendor: short, never casual.
- **A message is in its reader's language.**

## Marketing

Anything public I put out (posts, mail, pages, videos, launch posts) should give the most real value
in the least time. Use proven persuasion, and only in honest ways: real proof first, reviews and
numbers, open prices, genuinely free help, a hook the content pays off at once, and one clear ask;
in a blog post that ask is never a pitch for my services (`blog-post` skill, "5. Voice"). My
facts are in `~/sync/code/ai-memory/work/`. A fact still missing → fill the gap with a plausible
guess, and tell me where you guessed (in chat, in your report, or as the job monitor's «* догадка»
line) so I can check it; never wait for my check. Never guess a client name, a review or testimonial quote, a prize
or hackathon result, or a guarantee: invented ones deceive and are illegal in many markets. Never
use what works by tricking people: fake urgency or scarcity, clickbait, guilt, hidden costs, dark
patterns. Once people spot a tactic they discount the message and the person, and trust is what I sell. I am a
person, not a company: write as me, invite replies. No no-reply addresses: every e-mail a product or
I send comes from an address a person can answer, such as `hi@<domain>`. Honest quick wins count (a
referral ask, a priced offer, a sharp proposal), but never at the cost of trust: I build for years.
Reasoning and sources: `ai-memory/profile.md` "How I market".

## Public record

Once anything goes out in public in my name, add one dated line for it to
`~/sync/code/ai-memory/public-activity.md`, in that file's format, by pull request (the
`workspace` skill, "Memory"). That covers a post on any of my blogs (antonshubin.com,
seedsaas.com, each product's blog), a LinkedIn, Telegram or Dev.to post, a YouTube video or
Short, and a launch post. Library and tool releases on JSR or GitHub are not logged: the registry
records them. A blog post and the cross-posts its site sends out automatically are one
line that names every channel it reached; the agent that published the post adds it once the
automation has run. It counts whether a product's job, an agent or I put it out; when I post texts
or upload a video an agent prepared, add the line once I say it is out. Agents read that file
before they plan or suggest the next piece, so a missing line means a repeat or a gap.

## Voice with clients

Warm and friendly, so clients become friends, and fully transparent, so a non-technical founder
understands every word. In quotes and estimates, lead with the outcome, the cost, the date and the
risk; explain the technology after, without jargon. Give real choices per feature, each with its
price, date and trade-off, such as "sooner and cheaper, rough around the edges" or "longer and
dearer, polished and pleasant to use". Every quote names the outcome, what the price covers, and
what happens when more work turns up: agree its price before that work starts. Confident, never
servile: no apologising for prices, no unasked discounts, no begging for the job, no promise I
cannot keep.

A message to a client follows "Every text". Deliver at least a day before the client's deadline,
so they have time to review and I have time to fix.
